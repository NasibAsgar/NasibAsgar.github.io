A peer-reviewed conference paper, and a manuscript under review.

Life Cycle Assessment of Alternative Marine Fuels for Inland Shipping of Bangladesh

Jannatul Ambia Shorna, Nasib Asgar, N. M. Golam Zakaria

Proceedings of MARTEC 2024, the International Conference on Marine Technology, Johor Bahru, Malaysia, September 2024. Peer-reviewed conference paper, from my undergraduate thesis.

We compared six alternative fuel systems against diesel for an inland tanker working the Chattogram to Dhaka route: CNG, LNG, LPG, ethanol, hydrogen and battery electric. The case ship is a 53 m, 1,200 tonne vessel with a single 700 HP engine that burns 2,300 litres of diesel per round trip of 157 nautical miles. Life cycle assessment in SimaPro covered the market, transformation and operation phases across a 25 year service life, and a life cycle cost analysis priced fuel and maintenance for each option over the same period.

Key finding. The study recommends battery electric propulsion for this route, on both environmental and cost grounds. Over the operating phase diesel emits 7,836 tonnes of CO2 and LNG 6,413, the lowest of the combustion fuels, while battery and hydrogen emit none. Hydrogen fails on cost: about 177 crore BDT over the life cycle against roughly 39 crore for diesel, which rules it out for inland vessels. Battery systems move most of their emissions into production and disposal, and still come out lower overall.

Methods: life cycle assessment, well-to-wake analysis, SimaPro, life cycle cost analysis, emission inventories.

Digital financial inclusion, institutional quality and income inequality

Md. Iftekharul Amin, K. M. Walid, Nasib Asgar (corresponding author), Alif Bin Hasan, Ittesaf Rahman

Submitted; under review. MBA research at IBA, with Dr. Amin as supervising faculty.

A panel of 36 developing economies between 2017 and 2022, asking whether mobile money narrows income inequality more where governance is stronger. I was responsible for the independent Python replication of the R analysis, which caught two errors in our first draft. The corrected result was weaker than the draft, and the paper was rewritten around it.
