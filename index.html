Research

My research interest sits where computational fluid dynamics, thermal sciences, and sustainable energy systems meet. Naval architecture gave me mechanics applied to large structures moving through water, where thermal, structural, and economic constraints all apply at once.

My undergraduate thesis, published at MARTEC 2024, was a well-to-wake life cycle assessment of alternative fuels for Bangladesh's inland fleet. Its central lesson was that fuel rankings do not transfer between places: local supply-chain conditions reorder the carbon footprints, so a decarbonisation decision needs an analysis built for the local system. In a Ph.D. I want to work at smaller scales and higher fidelity, coupling CFD, conjugate heat transfer, and combustion modelling to design efficient thermal and energy-conversion systems.

I have taken a research question from framing through modelling to a peer-reviewed paper. My working tools are COMSOL, Abaqus, MATLAB, Python, and C++, which I have applied to heat transfer, FEA, hydrostatics, and data-analysis problems. The LCA work also taught me to turn environmental, economic, and regulatory data into engineering decisions, and I want to pair that habit with high-fidelity simulation.

I am most drawn to groups working on multiphase flow, thermal management, and clean combustion or energy conversion. I am also open to additive manufacturing and the control of energy systems.

Publications

Life Cycle Assessment of Alternative Marine Fuels for Inland Shipping of Bangladesh

Jannatul Ambia Shorna, Nasib Asgar, N. M. Golam Zakaria

Proceedings of the International Conference on Marine Technology (MARTEC 2024). Peer-reviewed conference paper, from my undergraduate thesis.

A well-to-wake life cycle assessment of candidate alternative fuels for Bangladesh's inland shipping, modelled in SimaPro against environmental-impact and techno-economic criteria, using supply-chain data, emission inventories, and regulatory standards.

Key finding. Fuel rankings do not transfer: local supply-chain conditions change the well-to-wake carbon intensity of each option. The study identifies the pathway with the strongest combined environmental and economic case for the inland fleet.

Methods: life cycle assessment, well-to-wake analysis, SimaPro, techno-economic evaluation, emission inventories.

Projects

Design of a 148 TEU inland container vessel

Completed Capstone design at BUET. Supervised by Prof. Dr. Md. Mashud Karim.

  • Found the principal particulars by iterative optimisation, balancing cargo capacity, hydrostatic stability, resistance, weight, and installed power.
  • Ran stability and hydrostatic analysis in Maxsurf and estimated resistance and powering requirements.
  • Produced structural and general-arrangement drawings in AutoCAD and Rhinoceros 3D.

Tools: Maxsurf, AutoCAD, Rhinoceros 3D. Topics: hydrostatic stability, resistance and powering.

Multilingual sentiment pipeline for mobile-finance app reviews

In progress Independent computational research, in Python.

  • Built a GPU-accelerated ensemble of three transformer models (XLM-RoBERTa family, with an mDeBERTa tiebreaker) over app-review corpora for five mobile-financial-service apps in Bangladesh.
  • Hardened it over several versions with stratified scraping, MinHash LSH deduplication, inter-rater reliability checks (Fleiss' kappa, Krippendorff's alpha), post-stratification, and difference-in-differences.

Tools and methods: Python, transformer models, reproducible pipelines, causal inference.

Mobile financial services and unplanned spending in Bangladesh

  • Examines present bias, financial literacy, and buy-now-pay-later use as drivers of unplanned spending.

Tools and methods: Python, factor analysis, regression and mediation, survey design.
