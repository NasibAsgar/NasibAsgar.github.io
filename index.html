Research statement

My research interest lies at the intersection of computational fluid dynamics, thermodynamics, and sustainable energy systems. While my background is rooted in naval architecture, my core focus has always been on the fundamental mechanical and thermal processes that drive large-scale transportation and energy networks.

In my published undergraduate research (MARTEC 2024), I conducted a well-to-wake life cycle assessment of alternative fuels. This work demonstrated how local supply chain conditions drastically alter carbon footprints, highlighting the need for rigorous systems-level thinking in engineering. Moving forward into a Ph.D. program, I aim to shift from macroscopic lifecycle models to microscopic, high-fidelity computational models. My goal is to couple CFD, heat transfer simulations, and combustion modeling to design highly efficient thermal and mechanical systems.

I am particularly interested in collaborating with research groups focused on multiphase flow, thermal management, additive manufacturing, or advanced control systems. I bring a robust quantitative toolkit—including COMSOL, Abaqus, MATLAB, Python, and C/C++—and a proven ability to synthesize complex environmental and mechanical data.

About

I completed my B.Sc. in Naval Architecture and Marine Engineering at the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET) in 2024 — one of the leading engineering institutions in South Asia, with a rigorous curriculum grounded in mechanical principles.

My undergraduate thesis investigated the life cycle assessment (LCA) of alternative marine fuels, a study subsequently published at MARTEC 2024. The research required synthesising environmental impact modelling, supply-chain economics, and regulatory frameworks to produce actionable guidance on decarbonisation.

Alongside my thesis, I completed a capstone vessel design project (148 TEU inland container vessel), gaining hands-on experience with iterative structural optimisation, hydrostatic stability analysis, resistance and power estimation, and professional CAD toolchains. My final-year GPA of 3.5 reflects a consistent upward academic trajectory across the rigorous engineering programme.
