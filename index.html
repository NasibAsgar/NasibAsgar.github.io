Focus

Research statement

My research interest lies at the intersection of computational fluid dynamics, thermal sciences, and sustainable energy systems. Naval architecture trained me in mechanical fundamentals applied to one of the hardest operating environments there is — large structures moving through water, under thermal, structural, and economic constraints at the same time.

My undergraduate thesis, published at MARTEC 2024, was a well-to-wake life cycle assessment of alternative fuels for Bangladesh’s inland fleet. Its central lesson was that fuel rankings are not portable: local supply-chain conditions materially reorder carbon footprints, so decarbonisation decisions demand rigorous, systems-level analysis. In a Ph.D. program, I want to move down in scale and up in fidelity — coupling CFD, conjugate heat transfer, and combustion modelling to design efficient thermal and energy-conversion systems.

What I would bring to a research group:

  • A demonstrated ability to finish and publish — I carried a research question from framing through modelling to a peer-reviewed paper while still an undergraduate.
  • A working quantitative toolkit — COMSOL, Abaqus, MATLAB, Python, and C++, applied to heat transfer, FEA, hydrostatics, and data-analysis problems.
  • Systems-level judgment — experience synthesising environmental, economic, and regulatory data into engineering decisions, ready to be paired with high-fidelity simulation.

I am particularly drawn to groups working on multiphase flow, thermal management, and clean combustion or energy conversion.
